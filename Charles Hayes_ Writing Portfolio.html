About This Portfolio

Ten published pieces from nearly seven years as ONTAP Product Marketing Manager at NetApp: five under my own byline on the corporate blog, three ghostwritten for senior engineering executives, and two on the NetApp Community platform. Every piece was written to do something specific: launch a product, claim competitive ground, or turn a third-party report into a narrative that lands with an IT decision-maker.

Skills This Work Demonstrates

Technical storytelling that doesn't lose the non-technical reader. A two-year content strategy built around sustainability across multiple product lines. The ability to write in the voice of a C-suite executive on material that has to be technically exact. Analyst relations copy that reads as informed perspective, not a press release. Those aren't separate skills; they're the same skill applied in different directions.

Section 1  —  Own Byline

netapp.com/blog

Five pieces on the corporate NetApp blog, 2020–2025. Sustainability is the dominant thread; I initiated and owned that content strategy. The collection also covers product launches, AI infrastructure, and data protection.

1
Unveiling NetApp SnapCenter 5.0
netapp.com/blog  ·  March 5, 2024  ·  Backup · Recovery · Data Protection
www.netapp.com/blog/unveiling-netapp-snapcenter-5-0/
Featured: NetApp SnapCenter, SnapLock, SnapMirror Business Continuity, ONTAP, Cloud Volumes ONTAP, Amazon FSx for ONTAP, Azure NetApp Files

Product launch blog for SnapCenter 5.0, covering five new capabilities: ransomware protection via SnapLock and tamperproof snapshots; zero-downtime backup and recovery with SnapMirror Business Continuity; extended cloud coverage to Amazon FSx and Azure NetApp Files; Linux file system protection; and tag-based VMware backup management. The structure is a listicle, but each item is framed around a business risk rather than a feature. Ransomware, downtime, cloud sprawl. The stakes stay visible throughout.

Ransomware protection is the most charged topic in enterprise storage right now. Framing each capability around a business risk rather than a feature spec was a deliberate call. That is what keeps this kind of content from reading like a release note.
2
NetApp HCI Inference at the Edge: Completing the AI Deep Learning Pipeline
netapp.com/blog  ·  May 7, 2020  ·  AI · Deep Learning · Edge Computing
www.netapp.com/blog/hci-inference/
Featured: NetApp HCI, ONTAP AI, FlexCache, NVIDIA TensorRT Inference Server

Published in May 2020, this piece starts with a problem that most AI marketing at the time ignored: data scientists weren't being slowed down by a lack of data, they were being slowed down by the infrastructure required to move it. From there it positions NetApp HCI and NVIDIA's TensorRT inference server as the solution for full edge-to-core-to-cloud AI pipeline mobility, with concrete use cases in medical imaging, manufacturing QC, and retail image recognition. The voice is more playful than the later pieces, with a pop culture hook in the opening and a looser structure. That was a deliberate choice for the audience.

Written five years before AI infrastructure became a marketing category. The subject matter expertise is genuine, not retrofitted.

The anchor piece of a two-year sustainability content strategy. The "you can't manage what you can't measure" thesis it establishes runs through everything that follows.

Section 2  —  Executive Ghostwriting

netapp.com/blog

Three pieces written entirely by me, published under the bylines of senior NetApp engineering executives. All three cover EDA storage benchmarks: the same technical category, three consecutive years, three completely different executive voices.

6
Rev Up Performance: NetApp Takes #1 Spot in EDA Benchmark
Ghostwritten for Octavian Tanase, SVP of Engineering, Hybrid Cloud Group  ·  May 4, 2023  ·  EDA · SPEC · Performance · AFF
www.netapp.com/blog/spec-bechmark-eda-solutions-top-spot/
Featured: NetApp AFF A-Series, ONTAP, FlexGroup, FlexCache, FlexClone

Written in the voice of NetApp's SVP of Engineering (Stanford MBA, Berkeley Math) to announce the #1 ranking in the SPECstorage Solution 2020 EDA_Blended benchmark: 75% more concurrent job sets than the nearest competitor. The business case runs through it: 70% of chip design costs are people costs, so storage that makes designers faster has a direct line to NRE spend reduction. From the benchmark result it expands into cloud bursting, ransomware resilience, data tiering, and workflow efficiency. The piece closes with a customer quote from NVIDIA's Director of IT Infrastructure. It earned its place.

The first of three consecutive EDA benchmark ghostwrites. Getting an executive's voice right on material this technically specific is a different kind of writing than product marketing copy, and it's harder.
7
Performance Triumph: New Platforms from NetApp Take Top Spot in EDA Benchmark
Ghostwritten for Krish Vitaldevara, SVP, Core Platforms  ·  July 31, 2024  ·  AFF · ONTAP · EDA Performance Benchmark
www.netapp.com/blog/platforms-take-top-spot-eda-benchmark/
Featured: NetApp AFF A90, ONTAP

Written in the voice of NetApp's SVP of Core Platforms to announce a 28% performance improvement over the previous AFF A900 result in the SPECstorage Solution 2020 EDA_Blended benchmark, with 80% fewer dedicated storage nodes than the nearest competitor delivering 28% more jobs. The piece explains the SPEC methodology credibly, walks through what drove the gains, and situates the result in the broader context of ONTAP's scalability and low-latency advantages for EDA workloads.

A different executive voice from the piece before it: different seniority level, different platform scope, different narrative angle, but the same technical rigour required.
8
Superior Performance with the NetApp AFF A90 for Demanding Data-Intensive Workloads
Ghostwritten for Pranoop Erasani, VP of Engineering, Shared Platform (AI/ML, NAS & Replication)  ·  May 13, 2025  ·  AFF A90 · ONTAP · EDA · Benchmarks
www.netapp.com/blog/aff-a90-all-flash-storage-system-performance-specstorage/
Featured: NetApp AFF A90, ONTAP

Ghostwritten for NetApp's VP of Engineering to announce the AFF A90's new top position in the SPECstorage Solution 2020_swbuild benchmark: 11,040 builds at 1.42ms response time, an 80% improvement over the predecessor AFF A900. The piece explains what the benchmark actually measures and why that matters for IT buyers in EDA and software build environments. That's not a given; a technical audience will check the methodology. It then traces the performance gain back to specific hardware and ONTAP innovations, and closes with a forward-looking executive perspective that sounds like a VP, not a marketing manager.

Three EDA benchmark ghostwrites, three consecutive years, three executive voices. Same technical category, completely different registers.

A Final Word

Ten pieces. Six years. Three platforms. Three executive voices I wrote in convincingly enough that none of the executives asked for revisions. One consistent standard: every piece has to earn the reader's attention in the first paragraph, or it hasn't done its job. I've held to that whether the subject was the mechanics of an EDA benchmark, a data protection product launch, or the inner workings of a 2020 AI inference pipeline that most marketing departments hadn't noticed yet.

My background is storage and enterprise tech marketing. I know enterprise storage, hybrid cloud, and AI infrastructure from the inside, not from reading about them.
